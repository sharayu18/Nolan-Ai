# SCRIPT — Soap Instantly Sinks Floating Needle
## @physicsexperimental | Night shoot | Paired format

---

## STEP 0 — ONE-TIME SETUP
*(Do this once before your very first shoot. Skip if already done.)*

**Canon XA11:**
1. Place a white A4 paper flat on your shooting table under the ring light
2. Point XA11 at the white paper, fill the frame with it
3. Go to Menu → White Balance → Custom WB → shoot the white paper → set
4. Note the Kelvin value shown — write it down
5. Keep XA11 in Manual (M) mode for all future shoots

**Nord CE5:**
1. Open Camera app → swipe to More → Pro (Video)
2. Tap WB → set to the same Kelvin value you noted from XA11
3. Go to camera Settings (gear icon) → turn on Grid Lines (3x3) and Level
4. Go to phone Settings → Sound → Default Recording Device → External Device
5. Connect DJI Mic Mini receiver via USB-C
6. Open DJI Mimo app → connect to receiver → set gain to -6dB for indoor
7. Set Noise Cancellation to Basic (L) on receiver switch
8. Do a 10 second test recording → play back → confirm mic is active not phone mic
9. Set Volume button action to Shutter (Settings → Buttons & Gestures)

---

## STEP 1 — SHOOT TIMING
**Tonight — Night shoot.**

Window light: Zero — completely dark outside. Do not use windows.
Ring light: Primary and only light source for everything.
Room LED: **Turn OFF.** Cool white LED will mix with ring light and create uneven colour cast on the water surface — kills the surface tension shot.
Phone torch: Keep nearby — may need it for one specific shot (detailed below).

---

## STEP 2 — OBJECTS NEEDED

For this experiment you need exactly these:

1. **One metal sewing needle** — standard size, not too thick, not too thin
2. **One shallow wide bowl or plate** — white or light coloured preferred. Wide enough that needle fits with space around it. Avoid dark bowls — water surface won't show up clearly on camera.
3. **One small piece of tissue paper** — to place needle gently on water surface without breaking surface tension
4. **One drop of liquid soap** — dish soap works best. Dettol handwash also works. Load one drop onto a toothpick or fingertip — do not pour directly.
5. **Water** — fill the bowl 3/4 full. Still water. Let it settle for 2 minutes before shooting — any movement in water will sink the needle before you want it to.
6. **One toothpick** — to carry the soap drop precisely to the water edge

---

## STEP 3 — OBJECT PLACEMENT

**Shooting table setup:**
Since Rishi has no fixed table — use any flat stable surface at a comfortable sitting or standing height. Cover it with a plain white or light grey cloth or paper if the table surface is dark or patterned. Dark background will confuse the camera's exposure on the water.

**Bowl placement:**
Place the bowl in the centre of the table.
Position it so the ring light is at approximately 45 degrees to the left of the bowl — not directly above, not directly in front. This angle creates a slight shadow on the water surface that makes the surface tension dimple (the dip the needle creates) visible on camera. Directly overhead ring light flattens the water surface and the dimple disappears.

**Needle placement:**
Do not place needle yet — place only when camera is ready and recording has started. Surface tension is fragile. Any vibration on the table after needle is placed can sink it.

**Soap on toothpick:**
Prepare this before pressing record. One small drop of dish soap on the tip of a toothpick. Set it aside within reach but not touching the bowl.

**Rishi's position:**
For experiment shots — sit or stand behind the bowl, hands visible in frame, face out of frame or at edge. This keeps focus on the experiment.
For on-camera CTA shot — step back from the table, face camera directly.

---

## STEP 4 — LIGHTING SETUP

**Night shoot — ring light only.**

**Ring light position:**
Height: Raise ring light stand to full height (5-6 ft). Tilt it downward at approximately 30-45 degrees toward the bowl. Do not aim it flat — aimed flat it creates too much glare on the water surface and the needle becomes invisible.

**Angle:** 45 degrees to the LEFT of the bowl (not directly behind camera, not directly to the side). This is your key light for all experiment shots.

**Diffuser:** Keep diffuser ON throughout. Without diffuser the ring light creates a harsh reflection ring on the water surface that distracts from the needle. Diffuser softens this to a gentle glow.

**Colour temperature of ring light:** Set to DAYLIGHT (neutral white) — not warm, not cool. Warm makes the water look yellow. Cool makes it look clinical. Neutral daylight makes the water look clear and the needle look like metal.

**Brightness:** Set to 70-80% — not full brightness. Full brightness at night overexposes the white bowl and clips the highlights. 70-80% gives clean exposure with room to see both the needle and the water detail.

**For the CTA shot (Rishi on camera):**
Move ring light to directly in front of Rishi, eye level. This is standard on-camera lighting. Diffuser stays on.

**Phone torch use:**
For the extreme macro close-up of the needle floating (Shot 2) — you may need a phone torch held at a very low angle (almost parallel to the water surface) pointing across the bowl. This creates a grazing light that makes the surface tension dimple dramatically visible. Ask someone at home to hold the torch or prop it on a book at table height pointing sideways across the water. Ring light stays on simultaneously.

## STEP 5 — CANON XA11 SETTINGS

*XA11 is used for the wide static shots where cinematic quality and sharpness matter most — specifically the needle floating overhead shot and the needle sinking wide shot.*

### XA11 Shot A — Needle floating overhead (0-8s)

**Frame composition:**
Bowl fills 70% of frame. Needle centred in bowl. Water surface visible around needle. Table edge not in frame. Rishi's hands may enter from bottom of frame when placing needle but exit before this shot begins.

**Shot type:** Top-down overhead. Camera directly above bowl looking straight down. Use the XA11's fluid head to tilt to full downward angle. This shot shows the needle floating on the surface — overhead angle makes the dimple (dip in water from surface tension) most visible.

**Aperture:** f/8 — everything in the bowl needs to be sharp. Needle, water surface, bowl edges all in focus. No shallow depth of field here — this is a documentation shot not an artistic one.

**Shutter speed:** 1/250s — bowl is static, no fast movement. 1/250 gives clean sharp image with no motion blur from any minor hand tremor.

**ISO:** ISO 200 — night shoot with ring light only. 200 gives clean image without grain. If image looks too dark at f/8 and ISO 200, open to f/5.6 before raising ISO.

**White balance:** Custom WB set earlier. Do not change.

**Camera movement:** Completely static. Lock fluid head. Any movement ruins the overhead shot geometry.

**Colour grading (post):** Cool neutral. Slight clarity boost (+10 in editing). Slight contrast boost (+15). Do not add warmth — water must look clear and clean. Needle must look like metal, not bronze.

---

### XA11 Shot B — Needle sinking (20-28s)

**Frame composition:**
Wide shot. Bowl centred. Rishi's hand with toothpick visible entering from right. Water surface and needle both clearly visible. Enough frame space to see the needle drop below the surface.

**Shot type:** Medium wide. Camera at table height or slightly above — not overhead for this shot. Eye-level with the bowl so we see the water surface at an angle. This angle makes the needle sinking more dramatic than overhead.

**Aperture:** f/5.6 — slight depth of field. Bowl sharp, background slightly soft. Gives cinematic quality to the reveal moment.

**Shutter speed:** 1/500s — the needle sinks fast. 1/500 freezes the sinking motion cleanly. This is the most important shot in the entire reel. Do not drop below 1/500.

**ISO:** ISO 400 — slightly more exposure needed at f/5.6 with this angle as less ring light hits the bowl directly. 400 is clean on XA11.

**White balance:** Custom WB — same as Shot A. No change.

**Camera movement:** Completely static. The drama comes from the needle moving, not the camera. Any camera movement here dilutes the reveal.

**Colour grading (post):** Same as Shot A — cool neutral, clarity +10, contrast +15. Consistency between shots is critical. If Shot A and Shot B look different in colour the edit will feel jarring.

## STEP 6 — NORD CE5 SETTINGS

*Nord CE5 is used for all close-up and handheld shots — needle placement, soap drop approach, and Rishi on camera.*

### Nord CE5 — One-time video settings before pressing record:

Open Camera → More → Pro Video
- Resolution: 4K 30fps (not 60fps — this is controlled indoor shooting, no fast motion until the sinking moment which XA11 captures)
- WB: Manual — same Kelvin as XA11
- Grid: On (already set in one-time setup)
- Level indicator: On
- Tap the needle on screen → hold until padlock locks → slide to adjust brightness so needle is clearly visible but bowl is not overexposed

---

### Nord CE5 Shot C — Needle placement close-up (0-3s)

**Frame composition:**
Extreme close-up of tissue paper carrying needle toward water surface. Needle fills centre of frame. Tissue paper visible. Water surface coming into frame from bottom. Bowl edge not visible. Rishi's fingertips holding tissue visible at top of frame — keep fingers clean, no nail paint distracting.

**Shot type:** Extreme macro close-up. Get the phone as close as possible to the bowl — 10-15cm distance. Use main 50MP lens — do not switch to ultrawide.

**Focus lock:** Tap needle on screen → hold until padlock appears → locked. Do not let it hunt.

**Exposure:** Slide brightness down slightly after locking — the ring light at close range can overexpose the white tissue paper.

**Resolution:** 4K 30fps

**OIS:** On (default) — stabilises the handheld macro shot.

**Noise cancellation on DJI Mic Mini:** Basic (L) — indoor controlled environment.

**Movement:** Extremely slow, completely steady push toward the bowl as the tissue lowers the needle. Any shake here ruins the macro shot.

**Colour grading (post):** Match XA11 shots — cool neutral, clarity +10, contrast +15.

---

### Nord CE5 Shot D — Soap drop approach (8-20s)

**Frame composition:**
Close-up of toothpick with soap drop approaching the water surface from the right side. Needle visible floating in background — slightly out of focus. Soap drop on toothpick is the sharp subject. Creates visual tension — audience sees soap approaching, knows something is about to happen.

**Shot type:** Tight close-up. Camera at water surface level — phone held horizontally at bowl height. This angle shows the soap drop and needle on the same plane which builds more tension than an overhead angle.

**Focus lock:** Tap soap drop on toothpick → lock. Let needle go slightly soft in background — this separation keeps focus on the soap as the drama-building element.

**Movement:** Extremely slow push in as toothpick approaches. Stop movement completely just before soap touches water — freeze the frame for maximum tension before the sinking.

**Resolution:** 4K 30fps

**Portrait mode (F icon):** Use here — set bokeh to f/4.0. Keeps soap drop sharp, needle floats softly in background. This is the one shot where bokeh adds to the story not distracts from it.

**Colour grading (post):** Same cool neutral. Slight shadows lift (+5) so needle is visible in the blurred background.

---

### Nord CE5 Shot E — Rishi on camera CTA (28-38s)

**Frame composition:**
Rishi from chest up. Centred in frame. Slight space above head — do not crop the top of head. Background: the experiment table visible but out of focus behind Rishi. Ring light moved to front eye-level position for this shot.

**Shot type:** Medium close-up. Standard talking head. Grid on — use rule of thirds, Rishi's eyes on the upper third line.

**Focus lock:** Tap Rishi's face → lock. Eyes must be sharp.

**Exposure:** Adjust brightness slider so face is well lit — not too bright (washed out), not too dark (shadowed). Ring light diffuser on keeps the light flattering.

**Resolution:** 4K 30fps

**Portrait mode:** On, f/4.0 — blurs the table background behind Rishi cleanly. Keeps him isolated as the subject for this shot.

**Movement:** Static. Rishi speaks, camera does not move.

**Colour grading (post):** Slightly warmer than experiment shots — +5 temp on Rishi's face looks more natural and human. Keep cool neutral for experiment shots, warm slightly for face shots. This is standard film colour practice.

---

## STEP 7 — DJI MIC MINI SETUP

**Connection:** Receiver plugged into Nord CE5 USB-C port. Already set Default Recording Device to External Device in Step 0.

**Transmitter placement:**
- Experiment shots (Shots C and D): Clip transmitter to Rishi's shirt collar or chest area. Rishi should not speak during these shots — audio is just ambient silence + music. Transmitter can be anywhere on body.
- CTA shot (Shot E — Rishi on camera): Move transmitter to shirt collar, 15-20cm from mouth. Omnidirectional pickup means it does not need to point at mouth — just needs to be close.

**Gain setting in DJI Mimo app:** -6dB for indoor night shoot with ring light. Rishi's voice at normal speaking distance will be clean without clipping.

**Noise cancellation:** Basic (L) — bedroom at night, fan may be running. Basic level reduces fan noise while preserving Rishi's voice naturally. Do not use Strong — it makes the voice sound slightly processed.

**Test before shooting:** Record 10 seconds of Rishi saying "Yeh dekho. Ek metal needle." Play back. Confirm voice is clear, no buzz, no hiss, no room echo. If echo is audible hang a bedsheet or blanket behind the camera to dampen room reflections.
